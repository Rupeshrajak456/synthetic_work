Testing the claim against 154 phrases

The claim makes a prediction about order: observation first, concealed evidence next, deduction and revelation last. Each figure below asks one question that could prove it wrong.

Show the data table

These tests are exploratory. Several expected counts fall just below 5, the usual threshold for a chi-square test, and occurrences in a single text are not independent of each other: a phrase like “the casket” recurs because the story keeps returning to it. The p-values show which patterns are strong enough to be worth reading closely, not that the reading is proved.

What the figures establish, and what they do not

Read together, the figures support the claim's order. Observation comes first, concealment gathers in the middle and again at the end, and knowledge is concentrated in the final stage. They also complicate it in three ways that the claim did not anticipate. The pearls are physically found in segment 10, before the reasoning that explains where they were is given in segments 12 and 13. Concealment peaks twice, once as events and once as explanation. And the object itself is the one constant, its share unchanged while its meaning shifts from possession to material to message.

What the figures cannot do is say why. Counts locate where to read; they do not read. That is the work of the interpretation.
